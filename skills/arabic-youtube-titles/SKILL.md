# Arabic YouTube titles

A title has two jobs: match what people type into search, and make the person who sees it want to click. The thumbnail text has a third job: say what the title does not.

## Inputs

- What the video actually shows and its single main promise.
- The search phrase people use (ask; or suggest 3 phrases and let the user pick). Arabic searches mix Arabic and English words ("مراجعة BYD Seal", "شرح Claude").
- Dialect of the channel and the country of the audience.
- Real numbers in the video (prices, days, results). Never invent them.

## Write 8 titles

Mix these patterns:

1. **Search first**: put the exact phrase people search for in the first 4 words.
2. **Result**: the outcome with a real number.
3. **Question** the viewer already asks.
4. **Comparison**: X و Y، مين الأفضل لـ…
5. **Mistake**: the error the viewer is making.
6. **Story**: first-person moment.

## Rules

- Under 60 characters so it is not cut on mobile; the key words go in the first 40.
- **No dashes and no slashes** (no "-", "|", "/"). Use a comma or "…" if you need a pause.
- English names in English letters (BYD, iPhone, ChatGPT); digits 0-9.
- The video must deliver the title. No fake shock.
- Do not repeat the thumbnail text inside the title.

## Thumbnail text

3 options, 2 to 4 words each, readable on a phone at a glance. It adds emotion or the result; the title carries the search words.

## Description

- First 2 lines: the promise and the search phrase, written naturally.
- Timestamps if the video is over 3 minutes.
- Links the creator provides, and nothing invented.
- 3 hashtags max.

## Output

A table of the 8 titles with pattern and character count, a star on the recommended one, then thumbnail options and the description.
