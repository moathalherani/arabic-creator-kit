# Arabic LinkedIn post

LinkedIn rewards posts that sound like a professional sharing something they learned, not an ad. Arabic posts on LinkedIn usually read best in clear, light Modern Standard Arabic with a dialect touch, unless the author wants full dialect.

## Gather (one question at a time if missing)

1. **The moment**: what happened (a project, a client, a mistake, a decision).
2. **The number**: a real figure from that moment (days, cost saved, people, result). Skip if there is none; never invent one.
3. **What changed in their thinking.**
4. **Who it is for** (engineers, founders, creators, job seekers).
5. **Language**: Arabic, English, or Arabic with an English summary.
6. **Confidentiality**: no client names, private numbers or internal details unless the author says they are public.

## Shape

```
Line 1: the hook (under 12 words; it must survive the "…more" cut)
(blank line)
2–3 short lines: the moment, with the number
(blank line)
The lesson, said plainly in one or two lines
(blank line)
3 short points (how to apply it), each one line
(blank line)
A real question to the reader
```

## Rules

- 120 to 220 words. Short lines; one idea per line.
- First person, specific, honest. Show the mistake as well as the win.
- No buzzword stacks (synergy, game-changer, "في عالم متسارع"…). No emoji walls; one or two at most.
- English product names stay in English letters; digits 0-9.
- No dashes or slashes in the first line.
- Max 3 hashtags at the end, relevant and specific.
- Do not tag or criticize companies by name unless the author asks and has the facts.

## Output

Give the post ready to paste, then two alternative first lines, then one sentence on the best time to post for the author's audience if they told you where that audience is.
